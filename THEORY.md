# Focus Field Theory

## 1. The problem

A finite subject always acts inside more information than it can fully hold. The practical question is therefore not how to represent everything, but how to locate a workable position:

> What field am I in, what is its center, and what can I validly do next?

Focus Field Theory proposes a reusable structure for answering that question across tasks, knowledge work, organizations, personal life, and—only as a hypothesis—other domains. It is first an engineered model of observation and action, not a claim that reality itself has already been proven to possess the same structure.

Its operational sequence is location, question formation, action, and revision. A subject first forms enough of the current field to locate its functional position. The field's unresolved closure gap then determines what is worth asking and what can validly happen next.

## 2. Field

A field is a temporarily stable structure formed relative to a contract:

\[
F = \langle \Gamma, G, K, \Pi, P, R, Cl, V \rangle
\]

- `Γ` — contract: goal, boundary, scale, evidence, constraints, success, failure, and tolerance;
- `G` — the typed relation field;
- `K` — the current center system;
- `Π` — necessary functional precedence;
- `P` — action and expansion frontiers;
- `R` — unresolved differences that affect the contract;
- `Cl` — relative closure conditions;
- `V` — versions and lineage.

A field is not a topic folder, a flat list, a single execution trace, or a complete map of the world. The same object may occupy different positions in different fields because its function depends on the governing contract.

## 3. Center

A center is the smallest functionally sufficient structure that cannot be removed without breaking the relevant closure obligation. It includes:

- indispensable functional positions;
- the conditions that make those positions necessary;
- the necessary partial order from entry to closure;
- typed joins for parallel branches;
- evidence and failure conditions.

“Smallest” and “sufficient” are always relative to a contract, scale, version, interface language, and tolerance. There is no absolute center and no permanently minimal representation.

Different tools or implementations may replace one another while occupying the same functional position. They are payload alternatives, not automatically different centers.

### Governing question

Once a field and a current center are located, Focus derives a governing question from the closure gap. The governing question is the smallest unresolved difference whose answer can change the selected center, the legal action frontier, or the current closure judgment.

The question may be directed to a person, evidence source, experiment, or the field itself. If the answer cannot affect the current position or next valid move, it is background curiosity rather than the governing question. If no unresolved difference requires an answer, Focus proceeds without manufacturing a question.

## 4. Necessary partial order

Only a strict functional dependency enters the necessary order:

\[
x \prec y \quad \text{when } y \text{ cannot validly hold unless } x \text{ already holds.}
\]

Support, similarity, conflict, competition, correlation, and temporal adjacency remain typed relations in `G`; they do not become precedence merely because they are related.

The order may branch. Incomparable branches have no required order between them. Their reunion requires an explicit rule such as `ALL`, `ANY`, `K_OF_N`, or a domain aggregation function. Parallelism is therefore not a disguised linear checklist.

## 5. Recursion and interfaces

Any necessary position may open into a child field when the parent cannot realize it directly:

\[
F_p[u] \xrightarrow{expand} F_c
\]

The parent passes a local contract and return obligation downward. The child forms its own field and center. When it closes sufficiently, it folds into a compact interface containing its result, evidence, assumptions, tolerance, residuals, version, and reopen condition.

Expansion substitutes the child structure into the parent dependency path; it does not hang an optional folder from the side. Compression hides detail without erasing the path needed to inspect or reopen it.

## 6. Dynamic address

An address records a functional position inside a particular field and version. It supports location, constraint, navigation, memory, and audit.

An address is not a permanent coordinate of an object. Stable object identity, field identity, address identity, address revision, field version, and panorama version remain separate.

Current address states are:

- `[+]` — realized with evidence;
- `[◇]` — calibrated and legal, but not yet realized;
- `[-]` — a proposed transition is prohibited by the current contract or order;
- `[∅]` — the current field has no faithful route for the relevant difference.

An uncalibrated hypothesis is not `[◇]`. A latent residual is a difference record, not an address. Addresses exist only when they perform structural work.

## 7. Relative closure

A field closes relatively when its current contract is satisfied:

- the closure endpoint holds;
- required predecessors and joins hold;
- required child fields have returned valid interfaces;
- the required frontier is empty;
- active residuals and interface error fit the declared tolerance;
- evidence is sufficient for the intended action or delivery.

Closure does not exhaust every possible detail. Legal but currently unnecessary frontiers and inactive differences may remain. Every closure certificate is bound to a field, scale, version, evidence set, and tolerance, so later evidence may invalidate it.

## 8. Difference, revision, and version

New evidence enters as a typed delta:

```text
ADD | REFINE | CONTRADICT | OUTSIDE | NOOP
```

The system finds the nearest affected position and reopens the smallest sound scope: a node, interface, field, branch, or ancestor. If a necessary predecessor fails, dependent realization and closure lose support. The old version remains immutable; repair produces a descendant version or an incomparable branch.

This is the operational meaning of stability being relative: continuity lies in the ability to locate change, preserve lineage, and form another usable closure—not in keeping one center forever.

## 9. Multi-center fields

A complete field may have several active centers, but several candidates, viewpoints, implementations, or parallel branches do not automatically form a multi-center field.

A true multi-center field requires at least two simultaneously active centers that:

1. carry different indispensable obligations under one non-empty field contract;
2. can close, fail, and reopen in independently identifiable ways;
3. are jointly governed by typed center relations and a closure rule;
4. cannot be replaced without loss by one interface-equivalent center.

A complex structure with one indivisible external closure identity is a composite single center. Independent contracts form multiple fields. Multiple parent obligations form multi-parent binding. These structures must not be inferred from visual branching alone.

Focus projects one selected center at a time. It retains the peer interfaces required to return to the complete field, but it does not load every center into one active execution surface.

## 10. Subject and self-construction

A subject is never a view from nowhere. It occupies positions in larger social, material, historical, and symbolic fields while also forming local fields for perception and action.

To construct a self is to make these relative bindings inspectable: which contracts are inherited, which centers currently organize action, which constraints are necessary, which are replaceable, what remains unresolved, and when a former closure must be reopened.

The theory does not prescribe one correct identity. It offers a way to locate and revise identity without assuming an absolute center.

## 11. Motion and stability

“Motion is absolute; stability is relative” is the theory's philosophical orientation. In the current engineering model it becomes a fallible maintenance rule:

\[
F_t^{stable} \rightarrow F_t^{open} \rightarrow F_{t+1}^{stable}
\]

Specific centers, addresses, payloads, and closures may change. The system attempts to preserve evidence, dependency, interfaces, and lineage through that change. It does not guarantee that every field has a unique center, reaches closure, or can be repaired in finite time.

The stronger ontological claim—that reality itself universally has this structure—remains open and would require independent predictions and evidence.

## 12. Focus as the operational method

The Skill implements four motions:

1. `FORM` — recover enough field structure to locate the current position and derive a governing question when one is needed;
2. `DRIVE` — choose exactly one necessary next move;
3. `REVISE` — let new differences invalidate and reshape prior closure;
4. `FOLD` — return a relatively closed, evidence-bound, reopenable interface.

The parent model still performs the task. Focus is a navigation, revision, and inheritance layer, not a separate authority and not a substitute for retrieval or domain verification.

## 13. Falsifiable program

The operational proposal is weakened when, under equal model, tools, information, and budget:

- independent analysts cannot produce comparable fields or centers;
- Focus-derived governing questions do not identify decision-relevant unknowns more reliably than direct questioning;
- Focus increases maintenance without improving task outcomes or error detection;
- simpler summaries, workflows, RAG, or knowledge graphs match its results at lower cost;
- incorrect centers persist longer because explicit structure locks them in;
- compressed interfaces cannot reliably restore evidence, assumptions, and reopen conditions.

The relevant measurements include field fit, governing-question relevance, next-move validity, drift, error, stale-version use, illegal dependency jumps, rework, closure quality, recovery after context resets, and total maintenance cost. The personal alpha tests question formation through observable behavior cases rather than a numerical leaderboard. Until controlled comparisons are complete, performance advantage remains a hypothesis.

## 14. Scope

Focus Field Theory currently provides:

- a structural vocabulary;
- a discrete event motion protocol;
- a tested state runtime for selected operations;
- an evaluation program.

It does not currently provide automatic truth discovery, a complete ontology, guaranteed center recovery, full multi-center scheduling, or demonstrated universal advantage.
